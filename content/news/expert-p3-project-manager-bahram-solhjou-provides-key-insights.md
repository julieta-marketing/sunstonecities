## Feature
Finding ways to reduce project costs and reducing the public risk are two top reasons for pursuing public-private partnerships (P3) according to Bahram Solhjou, the principal project manager at the Los Angeles County Internal Services Department.

Bahram was the featured speaker at Sunstone Cities’ P3 Strategy Series on Sept. 19. He has plenty of experience with P3 projects in Long Beach – he worked on the battery storage component of the Long Beach Civic Center rebuild and more recently helped put together a deal to install public EV chargers in the city.

“Governments look to public-private partnerships when there are capital needs,” Bahram said. “And there’s the matter of tax benefits – they can make a big difference for private investors. But the city or county can’t use them, so…”

Partnering with private developers can be more efficient, Bahram added. Some development groups can take a project from design through construction and operation.

That approach can reduce the government’s risk by putting the onus on the developer to complete the project on time and within budget.

“While the government always has responsibilities, this allows the risk to be shared,” Bahram said. “It provides a safety net.”

Governments considering a public-private approach have other considerations before signing an agreement. For example, there has to be an expectation the community will continue to see benefits after the contract has been completed – that the new asset will survive for continued use.

Interests of other stakeholders – the community residents, investors, etc. – have to be taken into account. If there is potential opposition, legal challenges have to be considered.

“You can’t ask a public agency to sign a contract when there is legal risk involved,” Bahram said. “The private sector is answering to its investors. The public sector is answering to its residents.”

And the final say for a project to go forward rests with elected officials – the City Council, the County Board of Supervisors. Arguments have to be strong to convince them.

There are significant advantages to P3 projects, Bahram said. They typically provide a quicker turnaround, and costs can be reduced through grants and other financial incentives available to the private sector.

“Sometimes, they private developers come up with better ways to get things done,” Bahram said. “But ultimately, it’s got to be a win-win scenario to work.”
