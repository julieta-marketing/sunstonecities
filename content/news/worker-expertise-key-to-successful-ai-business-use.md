## Feature

Artificial Intelligence is as ubiquitous in the workplace today as email was at the turn of the century – and that means hiring and training employees has to take AI into account.

That's the premise Roger Lloyd, Director of the Customized Training Institute for Artificial Intelligence at Rancho Santiago Community College District and Founder of Truedge AI, started with at the latest P3 Strategy Series sponsored by Sunstone Cities. Lloyd began working with Artificial Intelligence and Large Language Models in 2015, long before AI's arrival in the public consciousness in 2022 (in the form of ChatGPT).

## More Than a Tool

Lloyd stressed that AI – no matter which model you use – is more than a tool, but less than an answer machine. In other words, it is a resource that can save time and money, but only when used thoughtfully by humans familiar with the area they work in.

This awareness is key when training employees or learning to use AI yourself. AI is as effective – or ineffective – as the human using the tool.

"You don't use AI as a place for simple answers," Lloyd said. "It's a matter of asking 'and then.' You need to be looking for next steps."

## Becoming AI Literate

Lloyd pointed out that almost all startups and tech companies tout use of AI these days. It's now necessary to show how your team is "AI Literate," using AI models as colleagues providing feedback, using creative prompts with human creativity to discover solutions. The critical component to becoming an AI literate employee is knowledge of the business you're in, not the mechanics of AI use – something he calls domain expertise.

When training people in AI use, Lloyd said he makes it clear that the person is responsible for the result, not the AI model. He urged cross-checking of AI responses, including using multiple AI models to check each other's results.
